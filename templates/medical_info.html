Understanding Brain Tumors

Comprehensive medical information regarding the types, symptoms, diagnosis, and treatments of brain tumors.

What is a Brain Tumor?

A brain tumor is a mass or growth of abnormal cells in your brain. Many different types of brain tumors exist. Some brain tumors are noncancerous (benign), and some brain tumors are cancerous (malignant).

Brain tumors can begin in your brain (primary brain tumors), or cancer can begin in other parts of your body and spread to your brain (secondary, or metastatic, brain tumors). How quickly a brain tumor grows can vary greatly. The growth rate as well as the location of a brain tumor determines how it will affect the function of your nervous system.

Benign Tumors

Benign brain tumors do not contain cancer cells. They usually grow slowly and have clear borders that do not spread into surrounding tissue. However, they can still press on sensitive areas of the brain and cause serious health problems. Common types include Meningiomas and Pituitary adenomas.

Malignant Tumors

Malignant brain tumors contain cancer cells. They generally grow rapidly and invade surrounding healthy brain tissue. They can be life-threatening. Glioblastoma is the most common and aggressive type of primary malignant brain tumor in adults.

Symptoms & Diagnosis

Common Symptoms

  • New onset or change in pattern of headaches
  • Headaches that gradually become more frequent and more severe
  • Unexplained nausea or vomiting
  • Vision problems, such as blurred vision, double vision or loss of peripheral vision
  • Gradual loss of sensation or movement in an arm or a leg
  • Difficulty with balance or speech difficulties
  • Confusion in everyday matters or personality/behavior changes
  • Seizures, especially in someone who doesn't have a history of seizures

Diagnostic Methods

Diagnosis typically involves a neurological exam followed by imaging tests. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is the standard for evaluating brain tumors. Other tests may include CT scans, PET scans, and ultimately a biopsy to collect and test a sample of abnormal tissue.

Treatment Options

Treatment for a brain tumor depends on the type, size, and location of the tumor, as well as your overall health and your preferences.

Surgery

The goal is to remove as much of the tumor as safely possible without damaging healthy brain tissue.

Radiation Therapy

Uses high-energy beams, such as X-rays or protons, to kill tumor cells. Often used after surgery.

Chemotherapy

Uses drugs to kill tumor cells. Can be taken orally in pill form or injected into a vein.

Targeted Therapy

Focuses on specific abnormalities present within cancer cells to cause them to die.
